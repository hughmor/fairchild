## Part 1 — Landscape Research

### 1.1 The Verilog-A Open-Source Situation (Short Answer: Mostly No)

Neither **Verilator** nor **Icarus Verilog** support Verilog-A in any meaningful way. Verilator is architecturally a digital/RTL tool; iverilog has a dead flag for AMS that was never implemented. Open-source Verilog-A support comes from a different lineage entirely.

**OpenVAF** (GitHub: `pascalkuthe/OpenVAF`, Rust + LLVM) is the only modern, correct, open-source Verilog-A compiler. It compiles `.va` files to **OSDI shared libraries** (`.osdi`), a C ABI that any simulator can `dlopen`. The original repo is unmaintained since end-2023; the active fork is `arpadbuermen/OpenVAF-Reloaded` (on Codeberg), which bumped OSDI to v0.4. This is your compiler target.

**ADMS** (Qucs fork: `Qucs/ADMS`) is a legacy transpiler that generates per-simulator C++ from `.va` via XSLT templates. It is what Xyce currently uses. ADMS is slow, generates buggy code for complex models, and is not a viable foundation for new work.

**ngspice** (v39+, default in v43+) is the reference open-source OSDI consumer — it loads compiled `.osdi` models at runtime. Study its OSDI integration as the closest prior art.

**VACASK** (Codeberg: `arpadbuermen/VACASK`, C++) is a full analog circuit simulator written specifically to exercise OSDI. Presented at FOSDEM 2025. Its solver architecture (Newton-Raphson + GEAR + OSDI runtime) is the closest analog to what we are building, but in C++ and without any photonic support. **Port its architecture to Rust rather than re-deriving from SPICE textbooks.**

**Verilog-AMS 2023** is the current standard (Accellera approved March 2024). Verilog-A is its analog-only subset. The LRM is freely downloadable from Accellera. No simulator fully implements the full 2023 spec.

### 1.2 Commercial Tools — Publicly Known Algorithms & Features

#### Cadence Spectre (most relevant benchmark)
- **Analyses**: DC (Newton-Raphson + homotopy), transient (GEAR variable-order + trapezoidal), AC (small-signal), PSS (shooting-Newton), HB (harmonic balance), noise, PAC, PNoise.
- **Convergence**: Source stepping, GMIN stepping, pseudo-arclength continuation homotopy.
- **Verilog-AMS**: Spectre is the de facto reference AMS implementation. `.va` files compiled internally at runtime to optimized evaluation code. Automatic differentiation for Jacobian entries.
- **Photonics**: **Spectre Photonics Option** extends Spectre X to simulate photonic and electronic components in a single monolithic engine. No domain boundary; photonic compact models are just additional analog primitives with an `optical` discipline. PDK partnerships: AIM Photonics, imec, CompoundTek, GlobalFoundries. This is the commercial benchmark.

#### Synopsys PrimeSim / OptSim / OptoCompiler
- **PrimeSim HSPICE**: GEAR transient, shooting-Newton PSS, Backward Euler for initial DC, GPU/CPU heterogeneous acceleration. Native Verilog-A compilation at runtime. Gold standard for analog signoff.
- **OptSim**: RSoft-origin photonic system simulator. Time-and-frequency domain hybrid (passive subcircuits in frequency domain / S-matrix; active in time domain — this is the commercial state of the art for handling the carrier frequency mismatch). Models: lasers (rate equations), SOAs, electroabsorption modulators, AWGs, ring filters. MATLAB/CODE V interfaces.
- **OptoCompiler**: Unified EPDA platform. PrimeSim handles electronics; OptSim handles photonics; they share timestep. As of 2026 R1: Lumerical INTERCONNECT callable from OptoCompiler via PrimeWave. Lumerical CML Compiler can generate photonic Verilog-A targeting PrimeSim.

#### Ansys Lumerical
- **FDTD**: Maxwell solver (GPU-accelerated, multi-GPU since 2024 R2). Gold standard for nanophotonic device design.
- **MODE**: Eigenmode solver (EME) for waveguide design and S-parameter extraction.
- **INTERCONNECT**: Photonic circuit simulator. S-matrix cascade per wavelength. CML (Compact Model Library) format: foundry-encrypted, generated by CML Compiler from measurement/FDTD data.
- **CML Compiler**: The "Lumerical CML Compiler" automates generation of compact model libraries from measurement data. As of 2026 R1, outputs photonic Verilog-A targeting Synopsys PrimeSim — the first cross-platform photonic VA workflow.
- **Integration with circuit EDA**: Verilog-A export from CML Compiler → Spectre or PrimeSim. S-parameter Touchstone → any circuit simulator.

#### Keysight ADS / VPIphotonics
- **ADS**: RF/microwave simulator (HB, envelope, LSSp, circuit envelope). 2024: partnered with VPIphotonics for EOE co-simulation bridge within ADS.
- **VPIphotonics**: Time-and-frequency domain hybrid (same architectural approach as OptSim). Active elements in time domain; passive subcircuits collapsed to frequency-domain S-matrices called as black boxes. Bidirectional signal flow. Python/MATLAB/C++ extensible.

#### Siemens Questa ADMS
- Five simulation engines (Eldo for SPICE, Questa for digital) in one tool. Language-neutral: VHDL-AMS, Verilog-AMS, SystemVerilog, SPICE, SystemC. UVM-MS support.

### 1.4 Photonic Signal Representation

The fundamental challenge: optical signals oscillate at ~193 THz. A naive time-domain simulation would need sub-femtosecond timesteps — completely incompatible with circuit simulation.

**Universal solution — Slowly-Varying Envelope Approximation (SVEA):**
Remove the carrier; simulate only the complex envelope. An optical field `E(t) = A(t)·exp(j·ω₀·t + jφ(t))` becomes just `(A(t), φ(t))` or equivalently `(Re(t), Im(t))` — slowly varying at modulation bandwidth (GHz-scale), not carrier frequency.

**Representation in a circuit simulator:**
Encode the complex envelope on two "nodes" per optical port. Following MIT Sorace-Agaskar (2015) and the keikawa library, this is implemented as:
- `o_p` node: optical amplitude (or real part of complex envelope)
- `o_n` node: optical phase (or imaginary part)

With a corresponding discipline that has KCL (power flow) and KVL (phase) analogues. Existing Verilog-A models (keikawa) already use this two-wire scheme on electrical ports — they are directly portable.

**WDM multi-wavelength:**
Each WDM channel is a separate port pair `(o_λk_p, o_λk_n)`. Linear passive components don't mix channels; only nonlinear elements (SOAs, XGM) create cross-channel coupling.

**S-matrix augmentation:**
When a component has no time-domain model but has measured/simulated S-parameters (Touchstone `.snp`), represent it as an LTI system: convolve the input envelope with the impulse response (equivalent to the frequency-domain S-matrix in time domain). This allows passive components without analytical models to be incorporated.

### 1.5 Key Published Academic Work

1. **Sorace-Agaskar et al., *Optics Express* 23(21), 2015** — MIT. The foundational paper for Verilog-A EO co-simulation. Complete Cadence toolkit: ring resonators, WDM links, PDH locking loops. Defines the two-wire optical port convention.

2. **Chen et al., arXiv:2410.02282 / IEEE JSTQE 2025** — Demonstrates >64 Gbaud validated co-simulation of SiPh transceivers in Synopsys/Cadence/Keysight using interoperable Verilog-A models.

3. **Laporte et al., *Scientific Reports* 9:5918, 2019** (Photontorch) — Photonic circuits as sparse complex RNNs in PyTorch; GPU parallelism + backprop. The differentiable simulation precedent.

4. **"Optoelectronic Verilog-A model library," *Scientific Reports*, 2024** — Published open model library.

5. **"Wavelength-tunable equivalent circuit models for SPICE-based EO co-simulation," *J. Computational Electronics*, 2025** — Complex vector fitting (CVF) approach for accurate SPICE-compatible passive PIC models parameterized by wavelength.

## Part 2 — Architecture

### 2.1 Design Philosophy

The three differentiating choices relative to all existing tools:

1. **Time-domain first**: Unlike all open-source photonic simulators (which are frequency-domain S-matrix), we put the DAE solver at the center. S-matrix is available as a component type, not the simulation paradigm.

2. **Differentiable from the start**: Gradients flow through the simulation. Adjoint-method sensitivity analysis is designed in, not bolted on. This enables inverse design and parameter extraction that no commercial tool supports today.

3. **Rust core, Python API**: Memory-safe, high-performance core with a first-class Python interface for the photonics community (which lives in Python/Jupyter).

### 2.3 Optical Signal Discipline

The electrical analogy for optical signals:

| Electrical | Optical equivalent |
|-----------|-------------------|
| Voltage (potential) | Optical phase φ (rad) |
| Current (flow) | Optical amplitude A (sqrt-power, √W) |
| KVL | Phase conservation through passive lossless paths |
| KCL | Power conservation through lossless junctions |
| Impedance | Complex transfer function H(ω) |

Per optical port: two nodes — `(o_amp, o_phase)` or equivalently `(o_re, o_im)` for complex envelope. Optical components stamp contributions to both the amplitude and phase equations simultaneously.

This maps directly to the existing keikawa Verilog-A model library convention and the MIT 2015 paper convention — photonic models written for Cadence/ADS will port with minimal changes.

**Carrier frequency (wavelength) — third required parameter:**

The complex envelope is defined relative to a carrier frequency ω₀ (equivalently, free-space wavelength λ = 2πc/ω₀). This is not a nodal variable — it does not vary with time — but it is required by every optical device model to compute wavelength-dependent quantities:
- Propagation constant: β(ω₀) = 2π · n_eff(ω₀) / λ
- Waveguide group delay, dispersion, loss
- Directional coupler coupling ratio κ(λ)
- Ring resonator resonance condition

**Representation**: carrier frequency is a *connection attribute* associated with each optical port pair, not a separate MNA node. At netlist elaboration time, the elaborator:
1. Resolves ω₀ for each optical port from the driving source (laser model parameter)
2. Propagates it through passive connections via continuity rules
3. Checks compatibility at junctions (connected ports must share ω₀, or be explicitly cross-connected via a wavelength-conversion element such as a SOA under XGM)

**At simulation time**: ω₀ is passed to device model evaluation functions as a parameter (in `OsdiSimParas` for OSDI models; in a `SimContext` struct for Rust-native models). It is constant across all Newton iterations of a given simulation.

**WDM**: each WDM channel is a distinct port pair `(o_λk_re, o_λk_im)` with its own ω₀_k. The number of channels and their carrier frequencies are fixed at elaboration time. Passive linear devices have one transfer function per channel (evaluated at their respective ω₀_k); nonlinear devices (SOA, EAM) couple channels.

**YAML netlist representation**:
```yaml
laser:
  model: cw_laser
  ports: {o_p: optical_out_re, o_n: optical_out_im}
  params: {power_dbm: 0, wavelength_nm: 1550}   # ← sets ω₀ for this port pair
```
The elaborator reads `wavelength_nm` from the driving source and tags the connected net with ω₀ = 2πc / 1550 nm. All downstream devices on that net receive ω₀ automatically — they do not need to repeat the wavelength parameter unless they are wavelength-selective (e.g., a ring filter parameterised by resonance wavelength).

### 2.4 Differentiable Simulation Design

The adjoint method gives `∂L/∂p` for any scalar loss `L` and parameter vector `p` in a cost proportional to one forward simulation, regardless of the number of parameters.

**Design requirements:**
- Every `stamp!` call in device models records its symbolic dependency on parameters.
- The GEAR integrator accumulates the discrete adjoint via backward-in-time pass.
- OSDI models provide `∂f/∂x` (Jacobian, already required by Newton-Raphson) and `∂f/∂p` (parameter Jacobian, added to OSDI call interface).
- Python API exposes `.backward()` and `.grad` compatible with JAX and PyTorch autograd protocols via `custom_vjp`.

**Strategic differentiation**: SAX/Photontorch do this only for frequency-domain S-matrices. We do it for time-domain DAE — enabling gradient-based optimization of circuits with lasers, modulators, and nonlinear dynamics.

### 2.5 Device Model Abstraction — Unifying OSDI and Rust-Native Models

The MNA solver is entirely agnostic about where a device model comes from. Both OSDI-loaded models and models written natively in Rust implement the same `Device` trait:

```rust
pub trait Device: Send + Sync {
    fn num_terminals(&self) -> usize;
    fn setup_model(&mut self, ctx: &SimContext);
    fn setup_instance(&mut self, terminals: &[NodeId], ctx: &SimContext);
    // Evaluate device physics at current x; caches result for load_* calls.
    fn eval(&mut self, x: &[f64], flags: EvalFlags, ctx: &SimContext);
    // Stamp resistive (DC / quasi-static) KCL contributions into b.
    fn load_residual(&self, b: &mut [f64]);
    // Stamp linearised conductance (Jacobian) entries into the MNA matrix.
    fn load_jacobian(&self, mat: &mut MnaMatrix);
}
```

The netlist holds `Vec<Box<dyn Device>>`; the Newton-Raphson loop calls the same methods regardless of origin.

**OSDI wrapper**: An `OsdiDevice` struct wraps a loaded `OsdiDescriptor` pointer and manages model/instance memory (`Vec<u8>` of size `descriptor.model_size` / `descriptor.instance_size`). Its `eval` calls `descriptor.eval`, its `load_residual` calls `descriptor.load_spice_rhs_dc` / `load_spice_rhs_tran`, and its `load_jacobian` calls `descriptor.load_jacobian_resist` / `load_jacobian_tran`. The OSDI Jacobian pointer array (stored inside the instance memory at `jacobian_ptr_resist_offset`) is initialised once at `setup_instance` time to point directly into the `MnaMatrix` data — this is the same technique ngspice uses and eliminates a copy on the critical path.

**Rust-native models**: Compute everything inside `eval`, cache the Norton equivalent (G_eq, I_eq), and stamp in `load_residual` / `load_jacobian`. They use the same `stamp_conductance` / `stamp_current_source` helpers as the linear elements.

**Optical models via OSDI**: Authors encode optical ports as pairs of electrical nodes (SVEA convention). The `SimContext` passed to `setup_instance` and `eval` includes `omega_0` (carrier frequency) so the model can compute wavelength-dependent quantities (propagation constant, coupling ratio, etc.) without it being a nodal variable. For OSDI models, `omega_0` is inserted into `OsdiSimParas` before each `eval` call. For Rust-native models, it is a field of `SimContext` directly.

**No co-simulation barrier**: an OSDI BSIM4 transistor and a Rust-native ring resonator sit in the same `Vec<Box<dyn Device>>`, participate in the same Newton-Raphson iteration, and stamp into the same MNA matrix. Discipline checking (optical port connects only to optical port) happens at elaboration time, not at simulation time.

**Physics extensions:**
- Thermo-optic effect: `ΔT → Δn_eff = dn/dT · ΔT`; couples to a thermal network in the MNA
- Silicon plasma dispersion: Soref-Bennett model `Δn(ΔN_e, ΔN_h)`, `Δα(ΔN_e, ΔN_h)`
- WDM multi-channel: N channels as N complex envelope port pairs; coupling only through nonlinear elements

**Noise analysis:**
- Thermal noise (Johnson-Nyquist): resistors, waveguide loss
- Shot noise: photodetector, laser
- Relative Intensity Noise (RIN): laser
- Amplified Spontaneous Emission (ASE): EDFA, SOA
- Implementation: noise current/voltage sources stamped in AC noise analysis; correlate for optical noise

**Milestone**: End-to-end 64 Gbaud SiPh PAM4 transceiver simulation; eye diagram validated against published silicon photonics measurements.
